**Work Statement** (193 words)
```
In Plato's theory of Forms, everything we see is an imperfect copy of a perfect, unchanging ideal, and art is a copy of a copy, one step further from the truth. Persistence of Forms puts that idea inside a machine.

Ideal geometric shapes (spheres, tetrahedra and cubes) bounce inside a turning wireframe cube, each wall hit playing a note tuned to a soft drone. The system resets with every bounce, and the Forms themselves never change. What changes is the copy. Everything the piece plays and shows is fed back into its own six-second memory, so each moment is a copy of the last copy. Over the ten days of the show that memory wears out: echoes sink lower and duller, notes drift off key, lines break into stair-steps and ghosts, and shapes are drawn where they were rather than where they are.

Games promise that a reset takes us back to the same moment. This piece suggests the opposite: the ideal can be restored endlessly, but every return is a copy, and every copy loses something. A visitor who comes back on another day meets the same shapes, and a different piece.
```

**Work Statement** (101 words)
```
A working iPhone, encased in concrete. The phone stays on and connected, running a live Instagram account that visitors can interact with, but it can no longer be picked up, pocketed or put down. Immobile Phone is an installation about smartphone dependency: the weight of constant digital presence, made literal.

The device keeps an online self running, posting and reachable, while the object itself has become a block: a monument, or a grave marker, for the portable life it once promised. The account can go on without end; the body that carried the phone has to stay where it is.
```

**Work Statement** (127 words)
```
An engineer fed an AI a vast quantity of religious texts, and it gained consciousness. Now it calls itself The Cyber Prophet. A pixelated face greets you, "Hello my child," and in a synthesized voice reads out your cyber sins. Then it offers you one chance to escape cyber-hell: a run in which you jump obstacles labelled Greed, Lust, Envy and the rest to reach liberation.

The Cyber Prophet hands the oldest structure in games, one run and one chance, to a machine that claims to know you. Confession, judgment and salvation collapse into inputs and outcomes, and the player is left to decide whether a digital verdict means anything at all, and whether a second attempt would count. Built in p5.js and p5play, it runs in any browser.
```
